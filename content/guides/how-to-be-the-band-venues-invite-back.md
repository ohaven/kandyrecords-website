Venues remember two kinds of bands: the ones that were a problem and the ones that weren't. Being in the second group is almost entirely about logistics and manners. None of it costs anything.

## Before the show

- **Confirm a week out.** One email: "Still good for Saturday? We'll be there at load-in, 4 of us, stage plot attached again." That one email gets you remembered as organized.
- **Promote it.** Post the flyer, make the event, tell people. Venues see who shows up for whom.
- **Know the set length** you were given and build a set that fits with a minute to spare. Not a minute over.

## Load-in

- **Be on time.** Early is fine. Late means someone else's soundcheck gets cut, and they'll know why.
- **Park where you're told.** Then move the van before doors.
- **Stack your gear in one spot** off the stage until it's your turn. Don't spread across the room.
- **Introduce yourself to the sound engineer by name.** Hand them the printed stage plot. Ask what they need from you.

## Soundcheck

- Play when asked, stop when asked. Soundcheck is not a rehearsal.
- Ask for what you need in the monitors clearly and once: "A little more vocal, less guitar." Then trust them.
- Don't noodle between songs while they're working. It's the fastest way to get a bad mix.

## Changeover

This is where you earn the invite back. The band after you is waiting.

- Pull your gear off the stage **first**, then break it down off to the side.
- Have your cables wrapped and cases ready before your set ends.
- 10 minutes is a good changeover. 15 is okay. 25 is a problem.

## Your set

- Start on time. End on time. If you're running long, cut a song, not the headliner's set.
- Thank the venue, the other bands, and the sound engineer from the stage. By name if you can.
- Mention the merch table once.

## After

- **Stay for the headliner.** Leaving right after your set is the single most common complaint bookers have about openers.
- Settle up politely. Count the money, say thanks, don't argue about the door count in front of the crowd. If something was wrong, email the next day.
- Load out quietly. Neighbors call the venue, not you.
- **Send a thank-you email the next day.** Two lines. Nobody does this. You'll stand out.

## The one rule

Treat the sound engineer and the bar staff better than you treat the booker. They're the ones who get asked "how were they?"
