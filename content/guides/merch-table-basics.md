A $100 guarantee is nice. Selling twelve shirts at $20 is better. For most bands at this level, merch is the money, and the table is where the real show happens after the set.

## What to sell

Start small. Three things, done right, beat ten things done cheap:

- **One shirt design** in a black blank, sizes S–2XL. Order more M and L than you think.
- **Stickers.** Cheap, people buy two, and they end up on cars and laptops for years. This is advertising you get paid for.
- **Your music** in a physical form: CD, tape, or vinyl if you can afford it. People buy it to support you, not to play it. Sign it.

Add later: a second shirt, a hoodie for cold-weather runs, patches, a poster.

## Pricing

Round numbers. $20 shirts, $5 stickers (or 3 for $10), $10 CDs. Bundles move product: "shirt + CD, $25." Nobody wants to make change for $17.

## Take cards

A card reader costs almost nothing and more than pays for itself the first night. Square and similar readers plug into your phone. Also post a Venmo/Cash App QR code on the table for people whose card won't tap. Keep a small cash float ($40 in fives and ones) for the rest.

## Set the table up like a store

- **Table at eye level**: put a box under the cloth, or hang shirts on a rack behind you. Shirts flat on a table look like laundry.
- **Prices visible.** A sign, big. People won't ask.
- **Light.** Venues are dark. A clip lamp or battery LED makes a huge difference.
- **One person at the table during every other band's set.** That's when people are walking around with money. Rotate.
- **Mailing list signup**: a clipboard or a QR code to a signup form. Email addresses outlast every social media platform.

## Say something from the stage

Once, near the end of the set, clearly: "We've got shirts and stickers at the table back there, come say hi." Not five times. Once.

## Keep track

A phone note with the starting count and the ending count per item, per night. After a run you'll know what sells where, and you'll stop ordering 2XL white shirts nobody buys.

## Taxes, briefly

Sales tax rules vary by state and city. If you're selling in a state regularly, look up whether you're supposed to collect it. Not legal advice, just a flag so it doesn't surprise you.
