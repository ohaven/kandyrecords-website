Most first tours lose money. That's fine if you planned for it and not fine if you find out in a gas station in Utah. Here's the math, in plain terms.

## The costs

**Gas.** Total miles ÷ your van's real MPG × current gas price. A full van gets worse mileage than you think; use 12–14 mpg for a loaded passenger van and 8–10 for anything towing a trailer. Add 15% for detours and in-town driving.

**Lodging.** Count the nights. Price them honestly: $0 for a friend's floor, $90–140 for a cheap motel room split four ways. If you don't have a floor lined up, budget the motel.

**Food.** $15–25 per person per day if you're disciplined. $40 if you're not. Grocery store runs beat drive-throughs on both cost and how you feel on day nine.

**Merch to sell.** The upfront order. This is an investment, not a loss, but it comes out of the bank account before the tour starts.

**Vehicle.** Oil change before you leave. Budget $200–300 as a "something breaks" cushion. Something breaks.

**Misc.** Tolls, parking, guitar strings, drum heads, gaffer tape, that one toll road in Colorado.

## The income

**Guarantees.** Only count what's in writing. A booker saying "we'll take care of you" is $0 on the spreadsheet.

**Door splits.** Estimate low. If you don't know the room, assume 15 people at $10 split across 3 bands.

**Merch.** Take your average per-show merch sales at home and cut it in half for out-of-town rooms where nobody knows you. Then multiply by shows.

If the net is negative, you have three levers: fewer days, shorter drives (routing!), or more merch. You usually can't fix it by hoping for bigger crowds.

## Routing is a cost

Every tour has a drive that's too long. One. Not four. Plot the cities on a map before you confirm anything and reject any day over 6 hours unless the show is worth it. The gas you save by routing well is often the whole profit margin.

## Pay the band last

Set the rule before you leave: gas and lodging come off the top from a shared envelope or card, then merch costs get recouped, then whatever's left splits. Agreeing on this in the driveway prevents the argument in the parking lot.
